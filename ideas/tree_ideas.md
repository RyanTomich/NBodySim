## Morton sorting and radix/octree construction

Morton sorting assigns each particle an integer spatial address by normalizing its floating-point position within a bounding cube, quantizing each axis to `b` bits with boundary clamping, and interleaving the coordinate bits; the original `double` positions remain unchanged for force and moment calculations. Each three-bit group, read from most significant to least significant, selects an octant, so sorting `(Morton key, particle ID)` pairs makes every shared spatial prefix a contiguous particle range. A simple serial builder can recursively divide these ranges by the next three bits, stopping at the leaf bucket capacity or key-depth limit, and store nodes and child indices in preallocated arrays without repartitioning particle indices at every level. Radix means number base: a binary radix tree branches on individual bits, an octree on three-bit digits (base eight), and radix sorting is the separate digit-based sorting algorithm. Karras's parallel construction uses common-prefix comparisons to determine binary radix-tree ranges and children independently, then derives octree nodes at three-bit prefix boundaries. A 64-bit Morton key supports 21 complete octree levels, fewer than our current default of 32; duplicate keys require a shared leaf or further geometric refinement, since particle-ID tie-breaking adds no spatial resolution. Start with an uncompressed octree to preserve FMM's half-size parent-child scaling, verify that floating-point rounding preserves particle enclosure, and benchmark total simulation time and numerical differences before adopting the approach.
